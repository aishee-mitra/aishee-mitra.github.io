---
title: "The Optimism of the To-Read Pile"
date: 2026-08-14
excerpt: "My bedside table is a graveyard of good intentions and ambitious reading lists. I've finally stopped feeling guilty about it."
tags: [books, philosophy, curiosity, productivity]
---


I have a habit of buying books not for who I am, but for who I think I should be. There is a specific, intoxicating kind of optimism that comes with a new book—a belief that if I just spend the next few weeks immersed in this particular subject, I will emerge on the other side fundamentally changed, or at least significantly more interesting.

For a long time, my "to-read" pile felt like a mounting debt. I’d look at the stack of philosophy primers, technical deep-dives, and a few stray novels, and feel a genuine sense of failure. It was as if the books were judging me for my lack of discipline. I tried to fight it with a system: a spreadsheet, a strict reading schedule, and the "one book at a time" rule. I treated reading like a series of tickets to be punched, a quota to be met.

But lately, I've stopped trying to "solve" my reading list. I've realized that the guilt comes from a fundamental misunderstanding of what a personal library is. It's not a syllabus; it's a map. When I see a book I bought three years ago and still haven't opened, I no longer see a failure of will. I see a marker of a specific curiosity I had back then—a snapshot of what I was wondering about in a different version of my life.

I've moved toward a philosophy of *grazing*. Some days, I don't want a narrative arc; I just want a few pages of a manual on obscure synth architecture or a single chapter of a book on Stoicism. There is a distinct pleasure in jumping between three different worlds in a single afternoon. It’s a messy way to consume information, sure, but it’s how my curiosity actually works. I’m not interested in "completion" for the sake of it; I’m interested in the spark.

The pile is still there, of course. It might even be growing. But the weight has shifted. The stack of unread pages isn't a list of chores anymore. It's a collection of future versions of myself, waiting for the right moment to be invited in.
